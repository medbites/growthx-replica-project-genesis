Profile

Sri B.P. Haribabu is a versatile mridangam exponent from Chennai. He trained under the exemplary gurus 'Kalaimamani' Sri V.P. Ramadoss and 'Kalaimamani' Sri Thiruvaroor Bhakthavatchalam at the Tamil Nadu Government Music College, and was conferred the title 'Vaadhya Visharadha' in recognition of his mastery of the art.

Besides the mridangam he is equally proficient in pakhawaj, nattuvangam and rhythm composition. A specialist in playing for Bharatanatyam and Kuchipudi concerts, he is a sought-after mridangam artist who has accompanied many artists for over 25 years. Since 1993 he has travelled widely, with more than 85 tours abroad to around 20 countries, and has worked with major dance companies in the USA.

At Divyanjali he is the Secretary-Trustee and teaches laya, the rhythm of dance, as a subject of its own. He taught nattuvangam to Dr. S. Divyasena, his wife and artistic partner, and composes the jathis for her productions.

Try a tala

Every Divyanjali student learns to keep the tala with their hands. Press play and count along: clap, then the fingers, then clap and wave.

    A simple guide for counting. The real teaching happens in class.

    His book on rhythm

    Essence and Essentials of Rhythm is his book on the concepts of rhythm, written to nurture young artists. It is published by Divyanjali, alongside Dr. S. Divyasena's Duta Mahima.

    Fellow artists looked forward to it when it was announced at the school's Silver Jubilee.

    Frequently asked

    Who is Sri B.P. Haribabu?

    A mridangam artist, nattuvanar and rhythm composer from Chennai, titled 'Vaadhya Visharadha', and the Secretary-Trustee of Divyanjali.

    Who are his gurus?

    'Kalaimamani' Sri V.P. Ramadoss and 'Kalaimamani' Sri Thiruvaroor Bhakthavatchalam, at the Tamil Nadu Government Music College.

    Can he accompany our performance or arangetram?

    Yes. Send the date and venue through Book and Enquire.

    Does he teach rhythm to dancers?

    Yes. Laya is taught as a subject of its own to every Divyanjali student.

    What is nattuvangam?

    The art of conducting a Bharatanatyam performance with cymbals, reciting the rhythmic syllables that the dancer follows.
